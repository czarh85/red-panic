Red Panic · how it's made

How it's made

VUEngine

Red Panic is written in C on VUEngine, the Virtual Boy engine by Jorge Eremiev and Christian Radke, and fits a 2 MB cartridge.

One person, one AI assistant

One person directs the game: the ideas, the worlds, the choices, and the verdict on the console. An AI coding assistant (Claude) writes most of the code and the checks, through written roles: an architect plans, a coder applies, a tester judges. The intention is human.

Every place starts as a plan, with its depth planes and its idea, before it is built.

Tested in an emulator, then on the console

Every build is tested in an emulator before it is kept: scripted sessions play it, capture frames, and measure time and memory. The builds that count are flashed to a cartridge and played on a real Virtual Boy; what the console shows decides.

In numbers

Counted by a script from the git history, the session logs and the game, on 2026-10-16. The session figures are floors: only what the logs show.

  • The game: 8 planets of 5 places, 7 crossings, 46 music pieces (jingles included); the demo ROM uses 1,502,915 of the cartridge's 2,097,152 bytes (72 %).
  • The history: 1,647 commits, 860 lots, the plan rewritten 266 times, 68 passing test verdicts.
  • The sessions: 264 prompts written by a person, 163 hours of active sessions, 100 of them with the loop working alone.
  • The runs: 1,271 builds, 1,144 emulator sessions, 54 cartridge flashes.

The tools

  • VUEngine and its toolchain, for the game.
  • Mednafen, run headless, for the automated tests.
  • Python, for the checks, the plans and the pictures.
  • A flash cartridge and a Virtual Boy, for the last word.
